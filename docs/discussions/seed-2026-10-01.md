## 1. Q&A — NHL information floor: schedule Elo ~0.691, goalie quality adds 0 — what has anyone found beyond xG?

We gate NHL moneyline models on held-out log-loss.

**Setup:**
- Train season 2024-25, test season 2025-26, regular season only.
- The bar is fixed before any run: ≤ 0.6866, which is 0.010 under the home-rate baseline.
- Calibration bands are reported at ±5pp, n ≥ 100.

**What failed:**
- **Schedule-only Elo**, four variants: 0.6909 / 0.6921 / 0.6952 / 0.6907. All four use results and schedule only. The schedule-only floor looks like ~0.691.
- **Adding starting-goalie quality** (v5): 0.6912. Goalie quality was measured as shrunk, decayed goals saved above average per shot, applied as an Elo term from the as-of starter. The difference against v1 is −0.0003, so goalie identity added effectively nothing above team Elo. Starter coverage was 100% on the scored set.
- **For reference:** public benchmarks such as MoneyPuck sit around 0.648–0.661 log-loss. Our gate only certifies "better than schedule-naive", not market-competitive.

**What we're doing next:** shot-quality data. Play-by-play shot events (location, type, situation) feed an expected-goals model that is fitted once and frozen. Team rolling xG differential then replaces goal margin as the Elo input.

**Question:** beyond xG, what has moved held-out log-loss for you on NHL sides?
- Lineup or line-combination data?
- Special-teams splits?
- Travel and back-to-backs measured as distance and time-zone shifts, rather than flags?
- Anything that beat a goalie term?

Negative results are as welcome as positive ones.

## 2. Q&A — api-sports american-football 2025: home/away labels inverted for Aug–Oct? Anyone seen this?

An audit of our stored 2025 college football games (provider: api-sports, american-football product) found implausible home-field numbers:

| Season | Home win rate (FBS) | Mean home margin |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 0.404 | −6.05 |
| 2025, August only | 0.335 | — |
| 2026, same audit | 0.773 | +19 |

Home field in college football is normally well above 50%, so a 2025 rate below 0.5 looks like inverted home/away labels for part of the season. It could also be neutral sites mislabelled. It is concentrated early in the season.

**What we don't know yet:** whether the inversion is in our ingestion-era copy or at the provider. We're comparing the provider's current listing against our stored rows read-only before touching anything.

**Questions:**
- Has anyone seen home/away swapped in this provider's 2025 college data, especially August to October?
- Is it fixed in the current feed?
- Does it affect the NFL feed as well?

## 3. Ideas — Kalshi game markets: fill rates when joining the bid vs taking — who has data?

On Kalshi game-winner markets (NFL, NHL, college football, EPL, MLB) we compare two execution paths:

| | Price | Fee multiplier M |
|---|---|---|
| Join the bid (maker) | bid + 1¢ | 0.25 on the game series |
| Take the ask (taker) | ask | 1 |

- Fees use the published formula `0.07 · M · P(1−P)`, rounded to the cent per fill.
- MLB pre-live is M = 0.5 for both.
- The maker path is cheaper whenever it fills.

What we can't price from our side is **how often** a resting join-the-bid order fills before the price moves away. That depends on time to kickoff, spread width and market depth.

**Who has data?** We're interested in:
- fill rates for resting bids at bid+1¢, by time to start (T-24h, T-3h, T-60m, in the last 15 minutes);
- whether fills cluster near kickoff, and what the adverse-selection cost looks like, i.e. how far the price moves after a fill;
- differences between sports or series.

Aggregates are fine. Please share nothing account-identifying.
