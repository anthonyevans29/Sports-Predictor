- **Ops class, non-gate:** the exports contract and the Desk are unchanged; no model, threshold or call moves. The host's units and timers are untouched (item 2(d): their retirement is the cutover PR's).
- **Built (deploy/hosting/closing.py, the section "slate"):** after its closings, each tick of `closing-watch` runs `slate_watch` per family for the tick's US Eastern day.
  - Due: `slate_due` = the day's first kickoff (the family's stored, non-void games of the ET day, `day_games`) less 150 minutes, or 06:00 ET when that is later. A tick before it does nothing; a day with no game does nothing.
  - One run per family per day, from the receipts (kind `slate`, `slate_state`): a success or three attempts end the day; a refusal or a superseded run is no attempt (380.4, as `_attempts`). Under the lock the receipts are read again (a success or three attempts by then: superseded, nothing run).
  - The lock is the closing's (`acquire_lock`, the chain lock): a slate and a closing never run at once. Held through a 5-minute wait: a miss, the watch keeps trying.
  - The backup: today's, as a closing takes it (`todays_backup` / `take_backup`; a failed backup fails the attempt before any step).
  - The steps: `slate_steps` = `resolve_steps` (the closing's own resolution) for the day's games not started, every step word for word, the export step's `--out` sent to `exports/<slate file>`; SOCCER's export adds `--start`/`--end`. Names (`slate_file`, the family's `export_glob`): `nfl_predictions_<date>.json`, `mlb_MLB_<date>.json` (`--date <date>`, the closing's own), `soccer_PL_<start>_to_<end>.json`. The SOCCER window (`soccer_window`, sp_run.date_vars): a Saturday to Tuesday day takes the round of the Saturday on or before it (10-17 to 10-20 for Sunday 10-18, the file item 4 names); a Wednesday to Friday day takes the midweek override's form, the day to the day + 3.
  - After the steps: the file must be this run's (mtime), carry `desk_meta`, and hold a desk row for every game of the day not started when the steps finished (`missing_from_export` on the day re-read). Then the mirror push (label `slate`). Then ONE page, the card topic (three tries, ten seconds apart) and the screen. Any of them failing is a failed attempt, paged with the RUN BY HAND line. The file the export wrote stays in exports/ (as a prematch chain's does): the run pushes only once its checks pass, but a page that fails after that push has already pushed the file, and a file a failed attempt left in exports/ goes to the mirror with this machine's next push (the next closing's or slate's; the mirror copies every top-level exports/*.json).
  - Late (item 2(d), "a laptop asleep at the due time means the first tick after waking"): judged on the first tick that found the slate due, the earliest `queued_at` of the day's `slate` and `slate_miss` receipts (a hold's, a miss's and a refusal's included; a miss now records its tick and the due time); late when that tick came more than the watch's cadence (60 s) after the due time. A run held for a closing, or delayed by a miss, on a tick that came on time is not late. Its minutes are in the receipt either way.
  - The closings first (item 2(a): "The closing runs at T-35 are unchanged"). One watch process runs one thing at a time, so a slate started shortly before a closing's T-35 kept that closing's tick from starting (a moving-clock repro: the 13:00 closing queued at T-32 and paged at T-27.5 behind a 12:20 MLB slate). A slate now does not start while a closing the watch will still run, for any family of the tick, has its T-35 within `SLATE_GUARD_MIN` = 15 minutes (more than twice the longest any chain has held the lock, seven minutes, addendum 45 P6) or already past with its start more than five minutes off (`closing_ahead`; a family this machine refuses is none); its wait for the lock ends 15 minutes before that T-35. So held, it runs on a later tick, after the closing; the day's first hold is receipted (kind `slate`, `held`, the closing it waits for: neither an attempt nor a refusal, no page). A lock wait cut short this way is a hold, not a miss. Pinned on a moving clock (45 s a step): the cross-family case, the late wake at 12:24, a lock held 12:18-12:24, and a wait cut at the guard, each closing queued at T-35 and paged by T-30.
  - Misses (kind `slate_miss`, once per family, day and reason, notified, no attempt): the MLB feed unreachable (A6, as before an MLB closing; the watch keeps trying), the chain lock held through the wait, every game of the day started before a slate ran.
  - Refusals: the closing's own checks (`refusal_checks`). The family refusal (`SP_SKIP_FAMILIES`, MLB on the host) is receipted once per family, day and reason and pages nothing; any other refusal is notified once per family, day and reason (reading 7).
  - The closings: unchanged. Same groups, same steps, same files: a minute-by-minute test day pins them with the slate stage on and off. `notify_failure` gains an optional title (the slate's notices say "<FAMILY> slate"; every closing notice is as before).
- **The paging texts, as built (declared):**
  - Success: `SLATE <FAMILY> <YYYY-MM-DD>: <N> rows · PLAY <p> · PASS <q> · exports/<file>`; a late run adds the line `LATE <m> min: due HH:MM ET, run HH:MM ET`; the machine's line last (addendum 45). Priority default. N, p and q are the written file's rows and desk calls. Another call kind (soccer's LADDER) follows PASS by name (`· LADDER <k>`) and rows with no desk block follow as `· no desk call <n>`, each only when non-zero; neither is folded into PLAY or PASS.
  - Failure: `SLATE <FAMILY> FAILED: <step command> — RUN BY HAND: block_<MMDD>_<family>_slate.txt · attempt k/3`; the third attempt adds `· no further attempt will run today`; the machine's line last (the closing failure notice's shape). The step command is the failing step's line as the receipt records it (`python cli.py …`); after the steps: `export not written (exports/<file>)`, `exports/<file> carries no desk calls`, `missing from export (exports/<file>): match …`, `mirror push (exit N)`, `page (ntfy did not accept it in 3 tries)`, `backup (…)`.
  - Refusal (not the family's): `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> REFUSED: <why> — RUN BY HAND: block_…`. Misses: `SLATE MLB <date> MISSED: MLB feed unreachable: VPN on, Tailscale off — RUN BY HAND: block_… · the watch keeps trying`; `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> MISSED: the chain lock was held through the 5-minute wait — RUN BY HAND: block_… · the watch keeps trying`; `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> MISSED: every game of the day had started — no slate run`.
- **Receipts (item 2(c)):** kind `slate`: ts/host/release (append_receipt), family, date (ET), run_id, trigger, due, queued_at, ran_at, late_minutes, late, attempt, covers, file, rows, calls, backup, steps (as the closing records them), export_written (sha256), push, page, exit, seconds; failed and step when it failed, failure_notification. Every attempt's line carries the same keys, a lock that cannot be taken included (pinned per path). A hold: kind `slate`, `held`, `closing` {family, start, run_at}, exit 0. Kind `slate_miss`: family, date, reason, detail, queued_at, due, notified. The closing readers (`closing_state`, `_attempts`, `covered_ids`, `unpaged_called_off`, `last_calls`, coverage's groups) read kind `closing` only: a test pins them beside a slate success and a slate miss. `last_calls` reads the slate file as the morning file (an export no closing run wrote), as it read the prematch file. `sp_receipts` shows both kinds; a slate miss counts as failing.
- **coverage (item 2(b), deploy/hosting/coverage_map.py):** under each LIVE competition's line, `<CODE> SLATE · exports/<file> · exported <ET> · PLAY <p> · PASS <q>` (the newest file of the family's slate kind, by its own exported_at, carrying the Desk's calls and a desk row for every game of the day by match id); else `<CODE> SLATE · NO SLATE FILE — slate run due HH:MM ET`, or `— run block block_<MMDD>_<family>_slate.txt` where this machine refuses the family. The gap: `<CODE>: <n> GAMES, ROWS <r>, NO SLATE FILE`, claimed for a refused family too (it is item 1's alert). A file that cannot be parsed, or has no desk calls, is skipped with its reason on the line, never counted (law 4); a file whose name dates it more than 8 days before the day, or after the day after, is not read. With no file the line reads the watch's slate receipts (item 2(c): "the morning page reads them as it reads the closing lines"): where the watch runs no slate that day (a success, three attempts, every game started) it names the block, `— run block <block> (<why>: the watch runs it no more)`; else the due time with a failed attempt (`attempt k of 3 failed (<failed>): the watch tries again`) or a miss (`missed (<reason>): the watch keeps trying`) in brackets. `coverage()` carries it as `slate`. exports/ is read, never written.
