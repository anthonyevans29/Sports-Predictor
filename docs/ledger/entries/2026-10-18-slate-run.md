- **Built (deploy/hosting/closing.py, the section "slate"):** after its closings, each tick of `closing-watch` runs `slate_watch` per family for the tick's US Eastern day.
  - Due: `slate_due` = the day's first kickoff (the family's stored, non-void games of the ET day, `day_games`) less 150 minutes, or 06:00 ET when that is later. A tick before it does nothing; a day with no game does nothing.
  - One run per family per day, from the receipts (kind `slate`, `slate_state`): a success or three attempts end the day; a refusal or a superseded run is no attempt (380.4, as `_attempts`). Under the lock the receipts are read again (a success or three attempts by then: superseded, nothing run).
  - The lock is the closing's (`acquire_lock`, the chain lock): a slate and a closing never run at once. Held through a 5-minute wait: a miss, the watch keeps trying.
  - The backup: today's, as a closing takes it (`todays_backup` / `take_backup`; a failed backup fails the attempt before any step).
  - The steps: `slate_steps` = `resolve_steps` (the closing's own resolution) for the day's games not started, every step word for word, the export step's `--out` sent to `exports/<slate file>`; SOCCER's export adds `--start`/`--end`. Names (`slate_file`, the family's `export_glob`): `nfl_predictions_<date>.json`, `mlb_MLB_<date>.json` (`--date <date>`, the closing's own), `soccer_PL_<start>_to_<end>.json`. The SOCCER window (`soccer_window`, sp_run.date_vars): a Saturday to Tuesday day takes the round of the Saturday on or before it (10-17 to 10-20 for Sunday 10-18, the file item 4 names); a Wednesday to Friday day takes the midweek override's form, the day to the day + 3.
  - After the steps: the file must be this run's (mtime), carry `desk_meta`, and hold a desk row for every game of the day not started when the steps finished (`missing_from_export` on the day re-read). Then the mirror push (label `slate`). Then ONE page, the card topic (three tries, ten seconds apart) and the screen. Any of them failing is a failed attempt, paged with the RUN BY HAND line. The file the export wrote stays in exports/ (as a prematch chain's does): the run pushes only once its checks pass, but a page that fails after that push has already pushed the file, and a file a failed attempt left in exports/ goes to the mirror with this machine's next push (the next closing's or slate's; the mirror copies every top-level exports/*.json).
  - Late (item 2(d), "a laptop asleep at the due time means the first tick after waking"): judged on the first tick that found the slate due, the earliest `queued_at` of the day's `slate` and `slate_miss` receipts (a hold's, a miss's and a refusal's included; a miss now records its tick and the due time); late when that tick came more than the watch's cadence (60 s) after the due time. A run held for a closing, or delayed by a miss, on a tick that came on time is not late. Its minutes are in the receipt either way.
  - The closings first (item 2(a): "The closing runs at T-35 are unchanged"). One watch process runs one thing at a time, so a slate started shortly before a closing's T-35 kept that closing's tick from starting (a moving-clock repro: the 13:00 closing queued at T-32 and paged at T-27.5 behind a 12:20 MLB slate). A slate now does not start while a closing the watch will still run, for any family of the tick, has its T-35 within `SLATE_GUARD_MIN` = 15 minutes (more than twice the longest any chain has held the lock, seven minutes, addendum 45 P6) or already past with its start more than five minutes off (`closing_ahead`; a family this machine refuses is none); its wait for the lock ends 15 minutes before that T-35. So held, it runs on a later tick, after the closing; the day's first hold is receipted (kind `slate`, `held`, the closing it waits for: neither an attempt nor a refusal, no page). A lock wait cut short this way is a hold, not a miss. A closing that cannot start holds no slate (`slate_ahead`): while the MLB feed does not answer, an MLB closing is a miss on every tick, never a run (A6), so when an MLB closing would hold a slate the feed is probed once and, unanswered, the guard reads the other families' closings only (a feed-down MLB closing receipted as a miss had chained the hold through every MLB group of the day: an on-time MNF slate due 17:45 was held to the 20:15 kickoff and missed). An MLB slate checks its own feed before the guard, so a feed that does not answer pages its RUN BY HAND miss at once. Pinned on a moving clock: MNF 20:15 with MLB 18:35 to 20:40 and the VPN on from 17:30, the NFL slate runs at 17:45 and the closings and closing misses are the same with the slate stage on or off. The dry run leaves out of the guard the closings its own tick runs first (`after this tick's closings: …`) and, with no network, shows an MLB closing ahead both ways (`held if the MLB feed answers: …; if it does not, …`). Pinned on a moving clock (45 s a step): the cross-family case, the late wake at 12:24, a lock held 12:18-12:24, and a wait cut at the guard, each closing queued at T-35 and paged by T-30.
  - Misses (kind `slate_miss`, once per family, day and reason, notified, no attempt): the MLB feed unreachable (A6, as before an MLB closing; the watch keeps trying), the chain lock held through the wait, every game of the day started before a slate ran.
  - Refusals: the closing's own checks (`refusal_checks`). The family refusal (`SP_SKIP_FAMILIES`, MLB on the host) is receipted once per family, day and reason when the watch is run for that family (bare, or with `--family` naming it) and pages nothing; the host's unit runs `--family NFL --family SOCCER`, so no MLB slate receipt is written there, and coverage's `MLB SLATE · NO SLATE FILE — run block block_<MMDD>_mlb_slate.txt` line is the alert. Any other refusal is notified once per family, day and reason (reading 7).
  - The closings: unchanged. Same groups, same steps, same files: a minute-by-minute test day pins them with the slate stage on and off. `notify_failure` gains an optional title (the slate's notices say "<FAMILY> slate"; every closing notice is as before).
- **The paging texts, as built (declared):**
  - Success: `SLATE <FAMILY> <YYYY-MM-DD>: <N> rows · PLAY <p> · PASS <q> · exports/<file>`; a late run adds the line `LATE <m> min: due HH:MM ET, run HH:MM ET`; the machine's line last (addendum 45). Priority default. N, p and q are the written file's rows and desk calls. Another call kind (soccer's LADDER) follows PASS by name (`· LADDER <k>`) and rows with no desk block follow as `· no desk call <n>`, each only when non-zero; neither is folded into PLAY or PASS.
  - Failure: `SLATE <FAMILY> FAILED: <step command> — RUN BY HAND: block_<MMDD>_<family>_slate.txt · attempt k/3`; the third attempt adds `· no further attempt will run today`; the machine's line last (the closing failure notice's shape). The step command is the failing step's line as the receipt records it (`python cli.py …`); after the steps: `export not written (exports/<file>)`, `exports/<file> carries no desk calls`, `missing from export (exports/<file>): match …`, `mirror push (exit N)`, `page (ntfy did not accept it in 3 tries)`, `backup (…)`.
  - Refusal (not the family's): `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> REFUSED: <why> — RUN BY HAND: block_…`. Misses: `SLATE MLB <date> MISSED: MLB feed unreachable: VPN on, Tailscale off — RUN BY HAND: block_… · the watch keeps trying`; `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> MISSED: the chain lock was held through the 5-minute wait — RUN BY HAND: block_… · the watch keeps trying`; `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> MISSED: every game of the day had started — no slate run`.
- **Receipts (item 2(c)):** kind `slate`: ts/host/release (append_receipt), family, date (ET), run_id, trigger, due, queued_at, ran_at, late_minutes, late, attempt, covers, file, rows, calls, backup, steps (as the closing records them), push, exit; export_written (sha256) once the file is read, page once a page is tried, seconds on every attempt but a lock error; failed when it failed, step only when a step command failed, failure_notification. Every attempt's line carries the declared keys (family, date, run_id, trigger, due, queued_at, ran_at, late_minutes, late, attempt, covers, file, rows, calls, backup, steps, push, exit), a lock that cannot be taken included (pinned per path). A hold: kind `slate`, `held`, `closing` {family, start, run_at}, exit 0. A hold, a refusal and a superseded run carry item 2(c)'s keys too (family, date, rows, calls, file, exit; rows, calls and file null). Kind `slate_miss`: family, date, reason, detail, queued_at, due, notified. The closing readers (`closing_state`, `_attempts`, `covered_ids`, `unpaged_called_off`, `last_calls`, coverage's groups) read kind `closing` only: a test pins them beside a slate success and a slate miss. `last_calls` reads the slate file as the morning file (an export no closing run wrote), as it read the prematch file. `sp_receipts` shows both kinds; a slate miss counts as failing.
- **coverage (item 2(b), deploy/hosting/coverage_map.py):** under each LIVE competition's line, `<CODE> SLATE · exports/<file> · exported <ET> · PLAY <p> · PASS <q>` (the newest file of the family's slate kind, by its own exported_at, carrying the Desk's calls and a desk row for every game of the day by match id); else `<CODE> SLATE · NO SLATE FILE — slate run due HH:MM ET`, or `— run block block_<MMDD>_<family>_slate.txt` where this machine refuses the family. The gap: `<CODE>: <n> GAMES, ROWS <r>, NO SLATE FILE`, claimed for a refused family too (it is item 1's alert). A file that cannot be parsed, or has no desk calls, is skipped with its reason on the line, never counted (law 4); a file whose name dates it more than 8 days before the day, or after the day after, is not read. With no file the line reads the watch's slate receipts (item 2(c): "the morning page reads them as it reads the closing lines"): where the watch runs no slate that day (a success, three attempts, every game started) it names the block, `— run block <block> (<why>: the watch runs it no more)`; else the due time with a failed attempt (`attempt k of 3 failed (<failed>): the watch tries again`), a miss (`missed (<reason>): the watch keeps trying`) or, when the day's newest slate receipt is a hold, the hold (`held since HH:MM ET (<the closing it waits for>): the slate runs after it`) in brackets. The every-game-started miss counts as done only while no game of the day is still unstarted (the watch decides from the schedule, not from the miss: a game re-dated later into the day is slated on its next tick). `coverage()` carries it as `slate`. exports/ is read, never written.

- **Open for the architect (the build stands as written until ruled):**
  1. The feed-down guard's residual trade. With the MLB feed down, a slate may now start inside an MLB closing's guard (that closing cannot start). If the feed answers again while the slate runs, that closing starts on the first tick after the slate ends, late by at most the slate's run (a few minutes: four to ten steps). The alternative is to keep holding while a feed-down closing is ahead, which chains through every MLB group and misses the other families' slates (the finding this fixes). Which way?
  2. A bound on a hold, and a page for it. A hold behind closings that can start can still carry a slate past its family's first kickoff, and no page names a hold. Example (moving-clock harness, feed up): a getaway Wednesday, the laptop waking at 14:12; the SOCCER slate is held for the 15:00 closing and then for the MLB closings, and runs at 14:45, after the 14:30 kickoff. Proposed: when a slate is still held with no `SLATE_GUARD_MIN` window left before its family's first kickoff, notify `SLATE <FAMILY> <date> HELD for <closing> — RUN BY HAND: <block>` once. This is a new paging text, so it is not built. coverage's SLATE line now shows the hold.
  3. The LATE line's wording. `run HH:MM ET` is the first tick that found the slate due, not when the slate ran. Held, then missed, then run: `LATE 312 min: due 07:00 ET, run 12:12 ET` for a run at 12:41. Proposed: `LATE <m> min: due HH:MM ET, first tick HH:MM ET, run HH:MM ET` (the real run from the receipt's `ran_at`, printed only when it differs from the tick). This is declared paging text, so it is unchanged here (CLI.md says `run` is that tick).
  4. "Covers EVERY game" against a late slate (item 2(b)). A slate that succeeds after a kickoff cannot hold the started game: the export is `--status scheduled`, and the run checks only the games not yet started. So the page says success while coverage shows `NO SLATE FILE` and the gap for the rest of the day. Options: (a) coverage counts a file that holds every game not started at its `exported_at`, and names the started ones; (b) coverage also reads the day's successful slate receipt. Not built: the words are "EVERY game of the day".
  5. An older file covering the day. The newest covering file stands as the day's SLATE file whatever its date: Saturday's soccer window file on Sunday, or a Thursday or Saturday NFL file. The gap then stays hidden even after three failed slate attempts that day. Should the SLATE line need a file exported on the day, or read the day's slate receipts? And should the PLAY/PASS counts cover only the day's games? Not built.
  6. A failed attempt's file and the mirror (money path). The file a failed attempt wrote stays at its exports/ name, as a prematch chain's does, and goes to the mirror (with its latest/ pointer) on the machine's next push. It may have replaced an earlier good file of the same name, such as Saturday's soccer weekend file on Sunday. The alternative: write to a staging path and move the file into exports/ only once the checks pass. Which do you want?
  7. The SOCCER window. A Saturday to Tuesday day takes soccer-prematch's round window. A Wednesday to Friday day takes the day to the day + 3, the midweek override's form, which is a name soccer-prematch does not write by default. So a Friday PL game gets its own Friday to Monday file, because Friday's prematch window starts on Saturday. Is that the rule?
  8. What the counts count. LADDER (a staked call) follows PASS by name and is never folded into PLAY or PASS; rows with no desk block show as `no desk call <n>`. N, PLAY and PASS count the whole written file, which can hold more than the day (the soccer window; NFL's 36-hour default).
  9. Refused families and the gap. The gap line is claimed for a family this machine refuses (MLB on the host), as item 1's alert. The host never holds the laptop's MLB file in its own exports/, so the MLB gap would show there on every MLB day. Confirm.
  10. Built beyond the spec's words (rule each):
      - the `slate_miss` kind and its three misses;
      - the family refusal receipted but not paged, while other refusals are paged;
      - the third attempt's suffix;
      - the LATE line;
      - the machine line on the slate page;
      - default priority for the success page;
      - the 5-minute lock wait;
      - no slate once every game of the day has started;
      - the 15-minute guard (`SLATE_GUARD_MIN`) and the MLB feed probe it uses.
  11. `<N> rows` is literal: a one-game day pages "1 rows".
  12. A late slate after a closing. A slate that runs after a closing on the same day re-runs predict, which rewrites the stored prediction row of a closed game that has not started. The closing file stays the close.
  13. A game at 00:30 ET counts as that ET day's first kickoff. The day's slate is then due at the 06:00 floor, about 13 hours before a 19:08 first pitch, and the 00:30 game is in no slate.
