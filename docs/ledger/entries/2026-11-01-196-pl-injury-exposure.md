> 1. THE FINDING (from the operator's PL audit of 11-01, verified by the architect at main 0d3e84df): the soccer injury input counts EVERY stored feed entry as a player missing for the fixture. The feed is api-football's fixture-history injuries: `ingestion/service.py` keeps, per team, every entry whose fixture date is within 14 days past or in the future, of BOTH types the feed emits ("Missing Fixture" and "Questionable"; the type is stored, never read), wipe-and-replace per sync; `models/factors.injury_adjustment` then applies a position-weighted xG reduction per entry (0.06 attacker, 0.04 midfielder, 0.025 defender, 0.05 goalkeeper, 0.03 default), capped at 25%, with no regard to the entry's type or to WHICH fixture it names. So a player who missed a match ten days ago and is back, or who is "Questionable" and starts, is scored as absent. Saturday's receipt: Bournemouth 9 entries (−25% xG) and Leeds 5 (−15%) — the export's own name lists carried E. J. Kroupi (on Bournemouth's bench), D. James and N. Elvedi (started for Leeds), A. Stach (bench) — and the model priced Bournemouth at 25.6% against the market's 45%. The same mechanism touched Man City–Brighton, Spurs–Palace and Villa–Fulham. This is a candidate cause of the PL model's anti-predictive divergence from the market (13/30, behind the close; the floor-blocked big-edge rows 3–6), not a proof. Logged attributed in BACKLOG; an Issue (track:model, class:finding, sport:soccer, size:M).
